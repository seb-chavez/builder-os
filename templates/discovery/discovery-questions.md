## The rules this template exists to enforce

**A 30-minute call holds six to eight open questions.** Not twenty. Budget two minutes per question including their answer and your follow-up, two minutes of context at the top, and five minutes at the end for the ask. Anything past that is a list you will not get through.

**Past 30 minutes of material, everything gets a priority.**

- **P0**: ask it or the call failed.
- **P1**: only once every P0 is answered and time is left.

**The ask is always P0.** An introduction, a document, a next meeting, real data. It is the scarcest thing in the room and the first casualty of an overrun. Put it at the five-minute mark, not the last thirty seconds.

**Every question carries two lines.** **Why** we are asking, so it is not just a script and so whoever reads it later knows what it was for. **Listening for**, naming what a complete answer sounds like, so you know when you have it and can move on. Those two lines are what make the time-box hold.

**Ask what only they know.** Numbers, sequences, what actually happened last time. Anything answerable from a search engine or their marketing site is wasted minutes.

**Discovery, not a pitch.** If a question describes what you are building, it is not a discovery question. The last one should still be learning something, even when it is also the ask.

**Follow-ups are optional and exist for thin answers.** People answer plainly. "Yeah we did that, it was fine" is an answer that tells you nothing. Follow-ups reopen it by asking for the instance, the number, the person, or the counterfactual. **Skip them entirely when the first answer is already rich.** Using every follow-up on every question will blow the time-box, which is the failure this format exists to prevent.

Four follow-up shapes that reliably work:

| Shape | Example |
|-------|---------|
| The instance | "When was the last time that happened? Walk me through it" |
| The number | "How many? How long? What did it cost?" |
| The person | "Who actually does that, and is it their real job?" |
| The counterfactual | "What would have to change? What did you do instead?" |
