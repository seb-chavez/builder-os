# Discovery questions: time-box and prioritise

**Every discovery question list must be askable and answerable inside 30 minutes.**

That is six to eight open questions. Not twenty-five. Budget two minutes per question including their answer and your follow-up, two minutes of context at the top, five minutes at the end for the ask.

## Past 30 minutes of material, tag everything

| Tag | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| **P0** | Ask it or the call failed |
| **P1** | Only once every P0 is answered and time is left |

## Rules that make the time-box hold

**The ask goes in P0, phrased as discovery.** An introduction, a document, a next meeting, access to real data. It is the scarcest thing in the room and the first casualty of an overrun, so it cannot wait until the end. But it still has to be a question that learns something. Ask **what would have to be true**, not whether they will buy. "Would you do this with us?" is a close wearing a question mark; "if you wanted to change how this gets done, what would have to happen and by when?" surfaces the decision-maker, the gate and the deadline, and a warm answer opens the door on its own.

**Every question carries two lines.** **Why** we are asking, so it is not just a script and so whoever reads it later knows what it was for. **Listening for**, naming what a complete answer sounds like so you know when you have it and can move on. This applies to P1 as much as P0; a bare bullet list gives the asker nothing to steer by.

**Each P0 carries optional follow-ups for thin answers.** People answer plainly, and "yeah, we did that, it was fine" tells you nothing. Follow-ups reopen it by asking for the instance, the number, the person, or the counterfactual. They are optional by design: skip them when the first answer is already rich, because using every follow-up on every question blows the time-box.

**Ask what only they know.** Numbers, sequences, what actually happened last time, what they were surprised by. Anything answerable from a search engine or their own marketing site is a wasted minute.

**Write down the numbers first afterwards.** They decay fastest and they are the most valuable thing said.

## Why this exists

A 30-minute customer call once ran against an untagged list of roughly 27 questions. Too many questions with no priority meant no framework for the conversation, and it went nowhere. The questions were not wrong. There were too many of them and nothing said which ones mattered.
