# Brainstorming methods

Read only the method you are running. Each lists its steps, how to run it solo with the PM, and timings for a team workshop.

## Reverse brainstorm

**Use when:** the team is anchored on a solution already written up, or ideas keep coming back the same.

1. Flip the frame: "How might we make [the problem] worse?" or "How would we guarantee [the outcome] fails?"
2. List 10 to 20 ways to cause the failure. Bad, funny, and obvious answers are all fine.
3. Flip each one into its opposite. "Bury the receipt upload three screens deep" becomes "put upload on the first screen."
4. Keep the flipped ideas that are new; drop the ones that just restate the current plan.

**Solo:** the PM lists failure causes first, then the agent adds five more aimed at different parts of the journey.
**Workshop:** 5 min explain, 10 min silent failure ideas, 10 min share, 15 min flip in pairs, 10 min pick the new ones. About 50 minutes.

## Opportunity solution tree

**Use when:** the outcome is known and there are many possible ways to reach it. *Teresa Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits.*

1. Top of the tree: the outcome from the frame.
2. Opportunities: the customer needs, pains, and desires that, if addressed, would move the outcome. Ground them in research or interviews, not guesses.
3. Solutions: at least three per opportunity worth pursuing.
4. Assumption tests: for each promising solution, the riskiest assumption and the cheapest test of it.

**Solo:** build the tree level by level as a nested list. Ask for the PM's opportunities before proposing any.
**Workshop:** 10 min outcome and context, 15 min opportunities, 20 min solutions per opportunity, 15 min assumptions. About 60 minutes.

## Crazy Eights

**Use when:** you need many different ideas fast. *Google Ventures Design Sprint.*

1. Everyone folds a page into eight panels.
2. Eight ideas in eight minutes, one per panel. Sketches or one line each. No polishing.
3. Each person picks their top one or two and explains them in a minute.

**Solo:** the PM writes eight one-line ideas; the agent then writes eight that deliberately differ from the PM's (a different user, channel, or moment).
**Workshop:** 5 min explain, 8 min sketch, optionally a second 8-minute round, 15 min share. About 30 to 40 minutes.

## SCAMPER

**Use when:** improving something that already exists. *Bob Eberle.*

Walk the existing feature or flow through seven prompts, one at a time:

| Prompt | Question |
| ------ | -------- |
| Substitute | What part could we swap for something else? |
| Combine | What could we merge with another step, feature, or product? |
| Adapt | What works elsewhere that we could borrow? |
| Modify | What could we make bigger, smaller, faster, or more frequent? |
| Put to another use | Who else could use this, or for what else? |
| Eliminate | What could we remove entirely? |
| Reverse | What if we did the steps in the opposite order? |

**Solo:** one prompt per message, PM first, agent second.
**Workshop:** 5 min per prompt, 10 min to pick the best. About 45 minutes.

## User story map

**Use when:** shaping a new product or flow from start to finish. *Jeff Patton, User Story Mapping.*

1. Name the user types (primary first).
2. Backbone: the activities the primary user goes through, left to right, in order.
3. Under each activity, the tasks they do, most essential at the top.
4. Draw a line: everything above it is the first release (the thinnest path that works end to end).
5. Walk the map as each secondary user. Note where it breaks for them.

**Solo:** build the backbone with the PM, then fill tasks column by column.
**Workshop:** 10 min users, 15 min backbone, 20 min tasks, 15 min release line. About 60 minutes.

## Impact mapping

**Use when:** ideas must tie back to a business goal. *Gojko Adzic, Impact Mapping.*

Four levels, each answering one question:

1. **Why:** the goal (the outcome from the frame).
2. **Who:** the actors who can help or block it.
3. **How:** how each actor's behavior would need to change.
4. **What:** the deliverables that could cause that change.

Any deliverable that does not connect up to the goal is cut.

**Solo:** one level per message, PM first.
**Workshop:** 10 min goal, 10 min actors, 15 min impacts, 15 min deliverables. About 50 minutes.

## Six thinking hats (for converging)

**Use when:** pressure-testing the leading idea in step 4. *Edward de Bono.*

Look at the one idea through six lenses, one at a time:

| Hat | Lens |
| --- | ---- |
| White | Facts: what do we know, what is missing? |
| Red | Gut: how does it feel to users and the team? |
| Black | Risks: why might it fail? |
| Yellow | Benefits: what is the best case? |
| Green | Alternatives: what variation would be better? |
| Blue | Process: what do we decide, and what do we test first? |

**Solo:** one hat per message. The black hat's answers feed the brief's riskiest assumptions.
**Workshop:** 5 min per hat. About 35 minutes.
